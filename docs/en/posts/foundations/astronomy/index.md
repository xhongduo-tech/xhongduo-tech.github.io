# Astronomy

Covers the full content of a university general astronomy course, aligned with the chapter structure of Liu Xuefu's *Introductory Astronomy*. Check off each item once a blog post is written.

## Topic Plan

<ProgressGrid cat="foundations/astronomy" />


### Chapter 1: The Celestial Sphere and Celestial Coordinate Systems

- [ ] The concept of the celestial sphere and its fundamental points and circles (celestial poles, celestial equator, ecliptic, zenith)
- [ ] The horizon coordinate system and the equatorial coordinate system (hour angle, right ascension and declination)
- [ ] The ecliptic coordinate system and the galactic coordinate system
- [ ] Conversions between coordinate systems
- [ ] Precession, nutation, polar motion, and the diurnal apparent motion of stars

### Chapter 2: Time and Calendars

- [ ] Sidereal time and solar time
- [ ] Apparent solar time, mean solar time, and the equation of time
- [ ] Local time, zone time, universal time, atomic time, and Coordinated Universal Time (leap seconds)
- [ ] Julian Date and astronomical dating
- [ ] The computation of solar, lunar, lunisolar, and Chinese calendars (synodic months, leap months, the 24 solar terms)

### Chapter 3: Methods of Astronomical Observation

- [ ] Optical principles and types of astronomical telescopes (refracting, reflecting, catadioptric)
- [ ] Telescope performance metrics: aperture, limiting magnitude, resolution, and field of view
- [ ] Atmospheric windows, atmospheric extinction, seeing, and adaptive optics
- [ ] Radiation detectors: photographic plates, CCDs, and CMOS
- [ ] Photometric systems and magnitudes; foundations of astronomical spectroscopy
- [ ] Radio astronomy and space-based observations (infrared, ultraviolet, X-ray, gamma-ray)
- [ ] Foundations of astrometry: trigonometric parallax and the Hipparcos and Gaia satellites

### Chapter 4: The Earth–Moon System

- [ ] The shape, size, and rotation of the Earth
- [ ] The Moon's motion (synodic and sidereal months) and lunar phases
- [ ] The Moon's surface features, internal structure, and theories of its origin
- [ ] The principles and types of solar and lunar eclipses, and the Saros cycle
- [ ] Tides and tidal forces; a brief history of lunar exploration

### Chapter 5: Planets and Satellites of the Solar System

- [ ] The overall structure of the Solar System and Kepler's three laws
- [ ] The law of universal gravitation, the two-body problem, and orbital elements
- [ ] The Titius–Bode law and the classification of planets
- [ ] Terrestrial planets: Mercury, Venus, and Mars
- [ ] Giant planets: Jupiter and Saturn (including their rings)
- [ ] Distant planets: Uranus and Neptune
- [ ] Satellite systems: the Galilean moons, Titan, and Triton
- [ ] The internal structure, atmospheres, and magnetic fields of planets
- [ ] Detection methods and research progress on exoplanets

### Chapter 6: Small Bodies: Asteroids, Comets, and Meteors

- [ ] The asteroid belt, asteroid classification, and near-Earth asteroids
- [ ] The structure of comets: the nucleus, coma, and tails
- [ ] Cometary orbits and origins: the Oort Cloud and the Kuiper Belt, famous comets
- [ ] Meteor phenomena, meteor showers, and meteorite classification
- [ ] Pluto and dwarf planets

### Chapter 7: The Sun

- [ ] Basic parameters of the Sun, its internal structure, and helioseismology
- [ ] The Sun's energy source: the proton–proton chain and the CNO cycle
- [ ] The solar atmosphere: the photosphere, chromosphere, and corona
- [ ] Sunspots, flares, prominences, and the solar wind
- [ ] The solar activity cycle, magnetism, and solar–terrestrial relations (space weather, auroras)
- [ ] The solar neutrino problem

### Chapter 8: The Physical Properties of Stars

- [ ] Distances to stars: trigonometric parallax and the parsec
- [ ] The brightness of stars: magnitudes, luminosity, and distance modulus
- [ ] Spectral classification of stars (OBAFGKM) and the Hertzsprung–Russell diagram
- [ ] The sizes, masses, and mass–luminosity relation of stars
- [ ] Stellar motions: proper motion, radial velocity, and space velocity
- [ ] Stellar populations and chemical abundances

### Chapter 9: Stellar Internal Structure and Evolution

- [ ] Stellar structure equations, hydrostatic equilibrium, and energy transport mechanisms
- [ ] Star formation: gravitational collapse and protostars
- [ ] The main-sequence phase and main-sequence lifetimes
- [ ] Red giants, the helium flash, and the asymptotic giant branch
- [ ] Planetary nebulae, white dwarfs, and the Chandrasekhar limit
- [ ] Supernova explosions (Type Ia and Type II)
- [ ] Neutron stars, pulsars, and black holes

### Chapter 10: Binary Stars and Variable Stars

- [ ] Classification of binary stars: visual binaries, eclipsing binaries, and spectroscopic binaries
- [ ] Mass determination from binaries, mass transfer in close binaries (Roche lobes), and X-ray binaries
- [ ] Pulsating variables: Cepheids, RR Lyrae variables, and the period–luminosity relation
- [ ] Cataclysmic variables: novae and recurrent novae

### Chapter 11: The Interstellar Medium

- [ ] Interstellar gas, interstellar dust, and extinction and reddening
- [ ] Emission nebulae, reflection nebulae, and H II regions (Stromgren spheres)
- [ ] Neutral hydrogen regions and the 21-centimeter line
- [ ] Interstellar molecules and molecular clouds

### Chapter 12: Star Clusters and Nebulae

- [ ] Open clusters and their HR diagrams; cluster distance and age determination
- [ ] Globular clusters and Population II stars
- [ ] Stellar associations and famous nebulae

### Chapter 13: The Milky Way

- [ ] The overall structure of the Milky Way: the disk, halo, and the Sun's position
- [ ] Galactic rotation, the rotation curve, and spiral structure
- [ ] The galactic center and the supermassive black hole Sagittarius A*
- [ ] The formation and evolution of the Milky Way

### Chapter 14: Extragalactic Galaxies

- [ ] The discovery of external galaxies and the Hubble morphological classification
- [ ] Measuring galaxy distances: the distance ladder and Hubble's law
- [ ] The luminosities, masses, and mass-to-light ratios of galaxies
- [ ] Galaxy groups, clusters, and the Local Group
- [ ] Galaxy interactions and mergers

### Chapter 15: Active Galactic Nuclei

- [ ] Types of active galactic nuclei: Seyfert galaxies, radio galaxies, and quasars
- [ ] The discovery, basic characteristics, and unified model of quasars (supermassive black hole accretion)
- [ ] Jets, apparent superluminal motion, and gravitational lensing

### Chapter 16: The Large-Scale Structure of the Universe

- [ ] Galaxy surveys, superclusters, filamentary structure, and cosmic voids
- [ ] Weak gravitational lensing and mapping the distribution of dark matter
- [ ] The formation of large-scale structure: the growth of density perturbations

### Chapter 17: Cosmology: The Big Bang, Dark Matter, and Dark Energy

- [ ] The cosmological principle, the Friedmann equations, and the expansion of the Universe
- [ ] The cosmic microwave background radiation
- [ ] Big Bang nucleosynthesis and light-element abundances; cosmic inflation
- [ ] Observational evidence for dark matter and searches for candidate particles
- [ ] Dark energy and the discovery of accelerating expansion
- [ ] The ΛCDM standard cosmological model and the ultimate fate of the Universe

### Chapter 18: The Search for Extraterrestrial Life

- [ ] The conditions for life, the habitable zone, and habitable environments in the Solar System (Mars, Europa, Enceladus)
- [ ] Direct imaging and atmospheric spectroscopy of exoplanets
- [ ] The Drake equation, the SETI project, and the Fermi paradox
